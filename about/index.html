About

Who is writing this

Jains.es is written by Piyush Jain. I spent years as a product manager, most recently at Amazon. I know what language models do at the level a product manager knows it, the maths is rusty, and until this project I had never written a training loop.

I am learning to build one from scratch and publishing every session as it happens — the code, the sources, the working, and the days it does not work. Roughly one session a week fails. Those are published too, because a wrong turn explained is more useful than a result asserted.

Who it is for

People in India, across the Global South, and anyone for whom English is a second or third language. Where a topic has a cost, a language or an access angle, that is the angle taken, because that is the part nobody else writes.

The register is plain British English, short sentences, one idea at a time. No idiom, no jokes that need a culture to land.

How to trust any of it

Every number carries a dated link to its source, and the site build refuses to publish a page that cites nothing. Every wiki page says how finished it is — a seedling is rough and probably wrong in places, and says so at the top rather than pretending otherwise.

Corrections carry an explanation: what was wrong, how it got through, how it was caught. A silent edit tells you a page changed without telling you why the new version deserves more trust than the old one.

Everything is in the open — the plan, the curriculum, the prompts, the sources, and the code that builds the site. The method is as much the point as the result. The repository is here ↗.

Getting in touch

Reply to any newsletter, or email helloayursen@gmail.com. If you find a mistake, every page has an Edit this page link at the foot that opens the source file on GitHub. Corrections are the most useful thing you can send.

For anything longer than a correction — a question, a suggestion, an argument with something written here — GitHub Discussions is open ↗. Asking in public means the next person with the same question finds the answer.

The writing is CC BY 4.0 and the code is MIT. Translation is explicitly welcome — a good Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Swahili or Yoruba version of a page is worth more to the reader it is for than the original. Credit and a link back is the whole requirement.
