## What happened

**Your API bill in Hindi is roughly eight times your bill in English.** A
preprint published on 27 July measured it across fourteen Indian languages
and six tokenisers. Under `cl100k_base`, the tokeniser behind GPT-3.5 and
GPT-4, Indian languages pay an average 8.0x against English. Malayalam pays
13.0x. The same money buys you as little as 12% of the context window an
English user gets. ([arXiv 2607.24276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24276))

**A newer tokeniser cuts that cost by 73%, which tells you it was never
about the script.** The same paper ran XLM-R and OpenAI's `o200k_base` over
the same sentences and the average tax fell by almost three quarters. The
expense is a consequence of what the tokeniser was trained on, not of
Devanagari or Tamil.
([arXiv 2607.24276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24276))

**The Gates Foundation will spend at least US$1bn over two years to make AI
work in more languages.** The split, announced on 14 September alongside the
tenth Goalkeepers report, is 40% education, 40% health, 10% agriculture and
10% digital foundations. The release states that more than 90% of the data
used to train early large language models came from English-language sources.
That figure is the cause; the 8.0x above is the bill.
([Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/media-center/press-releases/2026/09/goalkeepers-report-equitable-ai))

**If you run racks in Nairobi, you have until early October to say what a
licence should cost.** Kenya's Communications Authority proposed a standalone
Data Centre licence on 9 September, separating co-location operators from the
Network Facilities Provider Tier-2 framework they sit under now. Public
comment is open for 30 days.
([Broadcast Media Africa](https://news.broadcastmediaafrica.com/2026/09/09/kenya-proposes-standalone-licence-for-data-centres/))

**One Kenyan short code now works across every network.** From 8 September
the Communications Authority allocates codes by service rather than by
operator, and content service providers can apply to the regulator directly
rather than negotiating with each network. It applies to newly assigned codes
only, so existing allocations keep their old terms.
([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/insight/techpoint-digest-1428/))

**Amazon has blocked Meta's shopping agent from buying anything.** Customers
using Meta's Muse agent on Amazon.com saw a notice on 21 September saying
continued access by an unauthorised AI agent violates Amazon's conditions of
use. If you were planning to let an agent do your ordering, the shop gets a
vote.
([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-ai-agent-has-been-blocked-from-using-amazon-com/))

## The intersection

**Where this lands: the price of a sentence.**

Take one sentence. Article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights,
which has official translations, so this is not one person's English against
another's machine Hindi. Send the English to a model's API and you are billed
for a certain number of tokens. Send the official Hindi and you are billed
for several times that.

Nothing about the meaning changed. The tokeniser cut the Hindi into more,
smaller pieces, because it had seen less Hindi when it was built.

This is why the 90% figure and the 8.0x figure belong in the same paragraph.
One describes what went into the tokeniser. The other is what comes out of
your card every month. A Bengaluru startup serving customers in Kannada is
not paying a premium for ambition. It is paying a premium for a corpus
decision somebody else made in 2022.

The fix is not on your side. It is a vocabulary, and vocabularies get
retrained.

## What I learned today

Day 1 of building language models from scratch, in public.

- A token is not a word and it is not a letter. It is a common piece of text
  that a tokeniser kept because it saw that piece often. If your language was
  a small share of what it saw, you get smaller pieces and more of them.
  ([Tokenisation and the cost of your language](/wiki/tokenisation-and-the-cost-of-your-language/))
- Tokens per sentence and tokens per character give different multipliers
  from the same table. I built a table that can be made to say two different
  things, which means I have to state which one I am using.
- **What I am least sure about:** my curriculum told me to expect a roughly
  4x difference. The paper reports 8.0x under the same tokeniser. I do not
  yet know whether that is my choice of sentence, my choice of metric, or a
  figure that has aged. I would rather say that than pick the flattering
  answer.
- The thing I did not expect: the gap is remediable. The 73% reduction under
  a newer tokeniser is the most useful number I read all day.

## Useful

**`tiktoken`** — OpenAI's tokeniser library, and the cheapest experiment in
this field. Install it, encode a sentence, take the length. That is the whole
measurement.

```
pip install tiktoken
```

Run it on a sentence in your own language and a translation of the same
sentence in English. The ratio is your number, not an average of fourteen
languages. It costs nothing and takes ten minutes.
([github.com/openai/tiktoken](https://github.com/openai/tiktoken))

## Number of the day

**900 million.** The number of Africans who live where mobile internet is
available and do not use it — about 60% of the continent's population.
Halving that gap in Ethiopia, Nigeria and DR Congo alone would bring 170
million more people online, according to a report from the GSMA and the
Partnership for Digital Access in Africa.
