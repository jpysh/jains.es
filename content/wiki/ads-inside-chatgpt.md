Amazon has launched a pilot letting advertisers buy placements inside ChatGPT through its demand-side platform, with text and image units appearing beneath ChatGPT responses and sold on a cost-per-click and cost-per-thousand basis, according to Retail Dive's report on 11 September 2026. Delta Vacations is among the first US brands testing it. For anyone with a retail media or performance budget, the significant part is the pricing model: an AI assistant now has buyable, measurable inventory with metrics your existing team already understands, which turns a strategic question about conversational discovery into a media test you can run this quarter.

Until now the honest answer to "should we advertise in AI assistants" was that there was nothing to buy. Brands could optimise for being cited, which is unmeasurable in any consistent way, or wait. A CPC unit bought through a DSP the team probably already uses is a different proposition.

## What you are actually buying, and what you do not know yet

The unit is a placement beneath a response to something a person asked in their own words. That is closer to search intent than display, and the query set will be nothing like your paid search keyword list, because people type differently to an assistant than to a search box. Whatever your team knows about keyword performance transfers only partially.

The open questions matter more than the format:

- **Attribution.** A click from a conversational placement lands in your analytics like any other paid click. What happens to the assisted conversions, where the assistant shaped the decision and the purchase arrives through another channel, is unresolved.
- **Frequency and context control.** Which conversations your brand appears next to is the brand-safety question in a new shape, and the controls in an early pilot will be coarser than what display buyers are used to.
- **Incrementality.** Some of this traffic would have reached you through search. Nobody has a clean read on how much, and the first advertisers in will be the ones who find out.
- **Volume.** Assistant query volume is large and the share of it that triggers a commercial placement is not disclosed. Budget capacity is unknown until you test.

None of those is a reason to wait. They are the reasons to spend a small amount now, because they are exactly the things that only a live test answers, and they will still be unresolved when the category is expensive.

## The case for a small test this quarter

The argument for early entry in a new channel is usually about cheap inventory, which is a weak argument because early inventory is also unproven inventory. The stronger argument here is organisational. A team that has run a conversational placement, argued about its attribution, and built one report on it has capability that takes a quarter to acquire and cannot be bought later. Teams that wait until the channel standardises will be buying at standardised prices with no internal expertise.

A reasonable shape for the test:

**Budget it as learning, not performance.** A figure small enough that the CFO does not need a forecast, and explicit about producing an attribution answer rather than a ROAS number.

**Pick products with unambiguous intent.** Categories where a person's question maps to a specific purchase give you the cleanest read. Considered purchases with long paths will not tell you anything in eight weeks.

**Set up the measurement before the spend.** Decide in advance how you will treat assisted conversions and what your holdout looks like. Retrofitting this is how a test produces a debate instead of a decision.

**Report the unknowns as findings.** The deliverable is a document saying what this channel does and does not let you measure. That is worth more than the conversions.

## What this says about where discovery is going

Amazon selling inventory inside a competitor's assistant is the notable structural detail. Amazon also made [Alexa+ free to Prime members](/blog/alexa-plus-loyalty-economics/), which is a bet on owning the assistant relationship. Doing both means the company is not assuming its own assistant wins, and is monetising the shift either way.

Retailers cannot do both. Most will not own an assistant, which means the practical planning assumption is that some share of discovery happens inside a third party's interface, and your options there are advertising and being retrievable. The advertising side now has a price. The retrievable side is a product data problem that nobody will sell you, and it is the one to start on if you only have capacity for one.
