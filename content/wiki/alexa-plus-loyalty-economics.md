Amazon has finished rolling out Alexa+, its agentic assistant, free to all US Prime members, with non-Prime customers getting either a limited text version or a $19.99 a month subscription, according to Retail Dive's report on 4 September 2026. In the same report, Amazon says shoppers who tried Alexa+ signed up for Prime at a nearly 25% higher rate, and that Q2 2026 subscription revenue rose 12% to $13.7bn. The disclosed conversion figure is the part worth taking to your own membership meeting, because a named retailer publishing a conversion delta for an AI feature is rare and it reframes the assistant as a paywall asset rather than a customer-experience project.

Note what Amazon did with the pricing. The assistant is free inside the membership and $19.99 outside it. That structure says Amazon believes the assistant's value is as a reason to hold the membership, not as a product with its own revenue line. A standalone AI subscription at that price would be a reasonable business. Amazon chose to spend it on Prime instead.

## Read the number carefully before you quote it

A nearly 25% higher signup rate among shoppers who tried Alexa+ is a real figure from the company that measured it, and it is also a figure about a self-selected group. People who try a new assistant are more engaged than people who do not. Some of that lift is the assistant working and some of it is the sort of customer who tries things.

That does not make it useless. It makes it a ceiling rather than a forecast. If your own programme tested something comparable and got a third of that lift on a similar population, it would still be one of the better-performing membership levers you have.

What the figure genuinely establishes is that an AI assistant can sit inside a membership value proposition and move the conversion rate at all. Before this, the honest position on assistants and loyalty was that nobody had published evidence either way.

## The question for your programme is what you have that is worth gating

Most loyalty programmes are built from discounts, points, free shipping and early access. None of those become more valuable when an assistant is placed in front of them, because the assistant does not change what the benefit is.

An assistant earns membership value when it does something that requires knowing the customer's history and account. That is a short list for most retailers:

- **Reordering and replenishment from actual purchase history**, including knowing what size, variant and quantity the customer buys.
- **Order and delivery questions answered without a service queue**, which is a cost saving and a retention benefit in the same feature.
- **Returns and exchanges handled conversationally**, where the friction is high and the customer's account already holds everything needed.
- **Availability and fit questions against past purchases**, which needs product data quality more than model quality.

Each of those depends on account-linked data that a non-member does not have, which is what makes it gateable. A general shopping chatbot is not a membership benefit, because a non-member gets the same thing from a general assistant for nothing.

## What to do this quarter

Pick the single highest-volume account-linked question your service team answers and cost what it would take to answer it conversationally for members only. Reordering and delivery status are usually the two candidates. The test is not whether customers enjoy the interaction. It is whether the members who use it renew at a higher rate than matched members who do not, which means setting up the measurement before you launch rather than reconstructing it afterwards.

Then decide your position on what happens outside your own channels. Amazon is building the assistant customers use, and separately [selling advertising inside ChatGPT](/blog/ads-inside-chatgpt/), which is a bet on both sides of the same shift in where discovery starts. Most retailers will not own an assistant. They will need their product and account data to be reachable by somebody else's, and that is a different engineering conversation from building a chatbot.

The trap is building an assistant because a competitor announced one, then measuring it on usage. Usage will look fine. Alexa+ is interesting because Amazon reported a membership outcome, and membership outcomes are the only reason a retail assistant justifies its cost.
